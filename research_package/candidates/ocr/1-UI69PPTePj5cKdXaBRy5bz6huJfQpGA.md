Mechanism of Diffuse Phase Transition in Relaxor Ferroelectrics

Takaaki TsURUMI, Kouji SoEJIMA, Toshio KAMIYA and Masaki DAIMON
Department of Inorganic Materials, Faculty of Engineering, Tokyo Institute of Technology,
Ookayama, Meguro-ku, Tokyo 152

(Received October 1, 1993; accepted for publication January 22, 1994)

Dielectric properties of lead magnesium niobate (PMN) and Ta-bearing strontium barium niobate (SBNT) were
measured as a function of temperature, and the crystal structure of PMN was refined by the Rietveld analysis
method. The results of structure refinement indicate that the volume of polar microregions (PMR) increases with
decreasing temperature. The dielectric properties of PMN and SBNT are well explained by an advanced theory of
dielectric dispersion. The anomalous behavior in dielectric permittivity (¢) observed on the low temperature side
of Tin, the temperature of ¢ maximum, is explained by simple dielectric relaxation, while on the high-temperature
side of Ty, is explained by the volume increase of PMR. It is concluded that these is no phase transition around Tm,
and the diffuse phase transition is an overlapping phenomenon of volume increase of PMR, freezing process of fluc-
tuating dipoles in PMR and dielectric dispersion around the measuring frequrncy. A model of diffuse phase transi-
tion and relaxor ferroelectrics is proposed. The difference between normal ferroelectrics and relaxor ferroelectrics

is discussed from the viewpoints of spreading of soft-mode phonons and disorder in the crystals.

1. Introduction

Ferroelectrics with diffuse phase transition, or relax-
or ferroelectrics, have been extensively studied due to
their technological importance and scientific interest.
They are characterized by a broad maximum in dielec-
tric permittivity (c) as a function of temperature,
together with a dielectric relaxation in the radio fre-
quency range. The model of diffuse phase transition
has been proposed by many research groups.') Most
of models proposed the formation of randomly oriented
polar microregions (PMR) in paraelectric crystals.”
The model proposed by Smolenskii et al.“ related the
formation of PMR to composition fluctuations. Cross et
al.” suggested that PMR thermally fluctuate at high
temperatures, and that the fluctuations have a freezing
temperature similar to spin glasses. Burns et al.*
found a glassy polar phase that exists in relaxor ferro-
electrics by means of measurements of optical index
refraction, Raman spectroscopy and other methods.
Other recent models took into account the concept of
ferroactive ions in the crystal structure.!">”)

The fluctuation and freezing of dipoles in PMR
should be detectable as a frequency dependence of di-
electric response (dielectric dispersion). Some
work®!3!5) has been done on the dielectric dispersion of
relaxor ferroelectrics but evidence of the fluctuation
has not been obtained. Moerover, most analyses of die-
lectric dispersion postulated the existence of a relaxa-
tion time distribution. This postulate seems to be
acceptable from the viewpoint of composition fluctua-
tions proposed by Smolenskii.?“) However, the depar-
ture from the Debye behavior observed in a vast range
of materials could be explained by advanced theo-
ries!©!” which do not postulate the relaxation time distri-
bution. As for crystal structure work, Bonneau et
al.'®") refined the crystal structure of lead magnesium
niobate over a wide temperature range and reported
disordered cubic structure of paraelectric phase.
However, uncertainty still exists in the relationship be-

tween crystal structure and electrical properties. In
order to understand the mechanism of diffuse phase
transition, it seems to be necessary to analyze electri-
cal properties and crystal structure of relaxor ferroelec-
trics from the viewpoint of the formation of PMR, ther-
mal fluctuation and freezing of dipoles in PMR.

We believe that the model of diffuse transition
should resonably explaine some important features of
relaxor ferroelectrics as follows: 1) Dielectric disper-
sion is observed on the low temperature side of ¢ maxi-
mum in the ¢ vs T curve. 2) The degree of diffuseness
of phase transition usually increases with decreasing
temperature of ¢ maximum (Tn). 3) € maximum is not
observed in optical frequencies.*" 4) Disordering of
constituent ions in the crystal structure is indispensa-
ble for diffuse phase transition.?°))

In the present study, the electrostrictive properties
of lead manesium niobate (PbMgi/sNb203, PMN) are
measured as a function of temperature, and the crystal
structure of PMN is refined by the Rietveld method.
Moreover, the dielectric dispersion of PMN and Ta-
bearing strontium niobate with tungsten bronze struc-
ture (SrosBaosNbieTao40s, SBNT) is measured, and
analyzed using an advanced theory of dielectric
response” in order to clarify the thermal fluctuation
and freezing behavior of PMR in relaxor ferroelectrics.
Finally, we will propose a model of diffuse phase transi-
tion which can explain various experimental results
reported for relaxor ferroelectrics.

3.6 Normal ferroelectrics and relaxor ferroelectrics

The formation of PMR begins at temperatures much
higher than Tn.*™ It is a precursor phenomenon of
phase transition from paraelectric to ferroelectric
phases. In these PMR soft-mode phonons are generat-
ed to yield fluctuating dipoles.

In normal ferroelectrics the soft-mode phonons can
easily spread throughout the entire crystal because of
the lack of disorder. The soft-mode phonons generated
before paraelectric-ferroelectric phase transition are at
the I’-point in the Brillouin zone,®” which means that
ions vibrate in phase in a crystal. The in-phase displace-
ment of ions over a long range generates a strong
Lorentz field. In a ferroelectric phase such as distorted
perovskite, the Lorentz field is in a direction which pro-
motes the displacement of ions.* Therefore, the soft-
mode phonons freeze at a certain temperature, which
creates a normal ferroelectric phase.

On the contrary, in relaxor ferroelectrics the soft-
mode phonons cannot spread throughout the entire
crystal, in other words, ions in the crystal cannot
vibrate in phase because of disorder in the crystals.
Although the volume of PMR increases with decreas-
ing temperature, their size is small and a strong
Lorentz field is not generated due to the size effect of
ferroelectrics. Subsequently, the fluctuation of dipoles
does not freeze at a certain temperature as in normal
ferroelectrics but the frequency of fluctuation
decreases with temperature according to a simple freez-
ing process. Furthermore, the total volume of PMR in-
creases with decreasing temperature without phase
transition. We concluded that this is the difference be-
tween normal and relaxor ferroelectrics and this is the
role of disorder in diffuse phase transition.

4. Conclusions

From the analyses of electrostrictive property, dielec-
tric dispersion and crystal structure of PMN, together
with that of dielectric dispersion of SBNT, we have
reached the follwing conclusions: 1) The volume of
PMR increases with decreasing temperature. 2) There
is no phase transition at T,, in relaxor ferroelectrics. 3)
The relaxation time distribution is not essential in ex-
plaining the dielectric dispersion of relaxor ferroelec-
trics. 4) Diffuse phase transition is the result of overlap-
ping phenomena of volume increase of PMR, freezing
of fluctuating dipoles and dielectric relaxation at meas-
uring frequency. 5) Soft-mode phonons cannot spread
throughout the entire crystal because of disorder, and
accordingly the precursor phenomenon (formation of
PMR) does not cause a phase transition in relaxor fer-
roelectrics.
