Sellmeier Coefficients and Chromatic Dispersions for

Some Tellurite Glasses

Gorachand Ghosh

Light and Radio Waves Section, Electrotechnical Laboratory, Tsukuba, Ibaraki 305, Japan

Sellmeier coefficients are necessary to optimize the design
parameters of optical devices. These coefficients are com-
puted for binary tellurite glasses to find the chromatic dis-
persion behavior at any wavelength, particularly at the
optical windows of optical fiber communication systems and
femtosecond technology. A single-oscillator DiDomenico
and Wemple’s dispersion equation is not sufficient to repre-
sent accurately the measured refractive indexes throughout
the transmission range. As a minimum, a two-pole Sellmeier
equation is necessary to represent the data more accurately.
Both the average electronic absorption band gap and the
lattice absorption frequency, lying in the UV and IR region,
respectively, affect the refractive indexes and their dis-
persion. Various other optical properties are discussed. A
single-oscillator dispersion equation is unable to verify the
existence of zero-dispersion wavelength.

I. Introduction

Set glass (SiO,) is the basic optical fiber material, and it
has been used extensively to make various kinds of optical
fibers and amplifiers as well as fiber lasers, with suitable doping
materials. Fiber lasers and amplifiers are the first step to replace
the older electronic regenerators and to lead to an all-optical
complete photonics age. To replace existing electronic switch-
ing, it is necessary to use optical switches to process light
signals without converting them to electronic form. These
switches are based on the nonlinear optical properties of materi-
als, i.e., the change of refractive index caused by an intense
optical pulse or by an applied electric field. Tellurite glasses
possess a high refractive index and high figure of merit for
photonic switches.' They can be used efficiently at the three
optic windows and may even be useful at the fourth optical
window of fluoride fibers.

The linear refractive index, its dispersion, and chromatic
dispersons are important characteristics of these glasses.
Knowledge of these characteristics is necessary for the evalua-
tion of optical fiber communication system designs using such
glasses in optical switches. In general, the refractive indexes are
measured with sufficient accuracy at discrete wavelengths in
the transmission region of the glasses. A suitable Sellmeier
equation is necessary to represent these data.

Recently, homogeneous glasses of the general formula
xM,,O,—(100 — x)TeO, in molar ratio were prepared and char-
acterized by Takebe et al.,” where M,,O,, was the network mod-
ifier. In this article, the measured refractive indexes of the above
tellurite glasses are analyzed and fitted, separately, in a single-
oscillator DiDomenico* and Wemple’s* dispersion equation and
in a two-pole Sellmeier equation.’ The various optical proper-
ties, such as Abbe number (v,), average electronic absorption
band gap (E,), zero dispersion wavelength (A,), and chromatic

Il. Derivation of Sellmeier Coefficients

Refractive indexes, a fundamental parameter of optical mate-
rials, are measured at discrete wavelengths with greater accu-
racy. There are various forms of Sellmeier equation to represent
the refractive indexes in the transmission region. Recently,
Takebe et al.? measured the refractive indexes of various binary
tellurite glasses with the experimental accuracy of +2.0 10°*
to +5.0 X 10°°. These data have been analyzed by using a
single-oscillator equation.’ But the optical constants were not
published, and even the accuracy of the fitting analyses was not
mentioned. A single-oscillator model is rewritten in a more
convenient form as

where nis the refractive index, B and C are optical constants,
is the wavelength in micrometers, and C is the average elec-
tronic absorption band gap. By using this model, the refractive
indexes are fitted for binary tellurite and SiO, glasses. The
optical constants are shown in Table I. It is clearly noticed from
this analysis that this model does not accurately represent the
refractive indexes.

On the other hand, recently, I have used the best physically
meaningful, wavelength-dependent, two-pole Sellmeier equa-
tion for representing the refractive indexes of some optical fiber
glasses and nonlinear crystals® with sufficient accuracy. The
Sellmeier is of the form

nr =A+B/(1 — C)’) + D/A — Ef’) (2)

where ) is the wavelength in micrometers. The first and second
terms represent, respectively, the contribution to refractive
indexes due to higher-energy and lower-energy band gaps of
electronic absorption, and the last term accounts for decrease in
refractive indexes due to lattice absorption. Normally, the initial
values of these coefficients are first calculated and then correc-
tions are added by an iterative process so as to minimize the
deviation between the measured and computed values. I am
able to evaluate the Sellmeier coefficients by fitting the mea-
sured data to an accuracy comparable to the experimental accu-
racy. In this way, I evaluate the constants A, B, C, and D,
taking beforehand a reasonably estimated value for E, the lattice
absorption frequency. The choice of the value for E is not
critical. The E value essentially determines the infrared trans-
mission cutoff and is a square of 15, twice the IR transmission
region’ of approximately 7.5 um. The computed Sellmeier

IV. Conclusion

The refractive indexes of binary tellurite glasses have been
fitted with sufficient accuracy in a physically meaningful, two-
pole, Sellmeier equation. The average electronic absorption
band gap and the lattice absorption frequency represent the
refractive indexes satisfactorily. The other optical properties,
such as Abbe number, average electronic absorption band gap,
chromatic dispersions, and zero-dispersion wavelength, have
been evaluated and are compared to those of SiO, glass.
